The problem is not simply "build an AI chatbot that takes medical history."

It is asking for a first-mile clinical intake system that sits before the consultation and does four major things:

This matches the problem statement extremely well. The official problem specifically calls for voice + touch history acquisition, adaptive questioning, AYUSH history, OCR/document intelligence, structured physician summaries, red-flag escalation, consent/privacy, and HIS/ABDM integration.

So yes — the core architecture is correct.

2. But your "hospital → doctor" idea needs one correction

You previously described:

Patient enters hospital → enters symptoms → system finds doctors and specialisations → sends report to concerned doctor.

This is conceptually good, but I wouldn't make "AI chooses a doctor" a central part of the product.

Instead:

Hospital integration

A hospital is onboarded into MediKiosk.

Hospital
   │
   ├── Hospital ID / HFR
   ├── Departments
   ├── Doctors
   ├── Specialisations
   ├── OPD schedules
   └── HMIS / EMR integration
             ↓
         MediKiosk

ABDM provides interoperable infrastructure and registries for facilities/professionals, but operational information such as current OPD schedule, queue, appointment and encounter status should come from the hospital's own system when integrated. ABDM is fundamentally about interoperable, consent-based exchange rather than being one centralized database containing all hospital operational data.

Therefore:

If the patient already has an appointment:

Appointment → assigned doctor

If they don't:

Complaint → suggested department → available doctors/OPD → patient/staff selection

Not:

AI diagnoses patient → AI chooses doctor.

That's an important distinction.

One final correction to the problem statement itself

There is one phrase I would not blindly implement literally:

"temporary session data is cleared immediately after submission"

because your entire longitudinal-history concept requires that some clinically relevant data from the encounter persists.

The correct architectural distinction is:

Temporary processing data
→ audio buffers, intermediate OCR images, temporary processing artifacts
→ deleted according to your retention policy.

Clinical record
→ consented structured history, verified documents, encounter, physician-confirmed diagnosis/prescription
→ retained as the patient's longitudinal clinical record according to hospital/legal retention requirements.

ABDM itself is designed around consent-based, decentralized health-information exchange rather than a central repository of everyone's records.

So don't say "we delete all session data."

Say:

"Transient processing data is minimized and purged according to retention policy, while the clinician-verified encounter record is retained with appropriate consent, access controls and auditability."

That is much more technically defensible.

Bottom line

Yes, your understanding is right. And after comparing our architecture directly against the actual SIH26047 requirements, I would confidently proceed with it.

But the final product should be framed as:

MediKiosk captures the patient's story before consultation, reconstructs relevant medical history from voice and documents, creates an evidence-linked clinical story, routes it into the hospital workflow, gives the physician a verified pre-consultation brief, and then turns the completed encounter into longitudinal history for future visits.

That covers the complete SIH problem, rather than solving only the "AI history-taking" portion.

And importantly, it gives you a coherent answer to the judge's likely question:

"What happens after the patient finishes talking?"

Your answer becomes:

"MediKiosk doesn't stop at a summary. It creates an encounter, routes the clinical story into the hospital workflow, supports the doctor's consultation, captures the verified outcome, and carries that history forward to the patient's next visit."

That is the architecture I'd now use as the master architecture for your UI, workflow diagram, technical architecture, database design and demo.
